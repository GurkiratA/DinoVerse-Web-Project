Evidence Over Hype

Movies make dinosaurs look huge, loud, and dramatic. Science looks at evidence, rock layers, and careful study. This page combines both ideas. You will find dinosaur discoveries from different continents, myth-busting based on museum evidence, and interactive mini-games.

Map of Discoveries & Continental Drift

The Earth in the Mesozoic looked very different from today. The continents were once joined as Pangaea and later split apart. The Atlantic Ocean grew wider, and India eventually collided with Asia. Because of this, dinosaur fossils are found on different continents today not because dinosaurs traveled between them, but because the continents moved. Similar rock layers and related species on separate landmasses helped scientists understand plate tectonics. For example, fossils like Spinosaurus in North Africa and Giganotosaurus in Argentina show that these regions were once connected.

Dinosaur Facts & Myths

Feathers vs Scales

Myth: Every dinosaur was fully feathered like modern birds.
Fact: Many theropods had feathers or simple filament-like structures. Feather coverage likely varied depending on the species, age, and environment, and larger dinosaurs may have had less body covering than smaller ones.

Tyrannosaurus rex Speed

Myth: T-Rex could run at extremely high speeds like a modern fast predator.
Fact: Studies suggest T-Rex could walk efficiently and may have been capable of short bursts of speed, but not sustained high-speed running.

Extinction

Myth: A single cause explains the extinction of dinosaurs.
Fact: Non-avian dinosaurs went extinct at the end of the Cretaceous, and scientists continue to study multiple contributing factors such as an asteroid impact, volcanic activity, and environmental changes.

Prehistoric Non-Dinosaurs

People often use the word “dinosaur” for anything old and reptile-like, but in science the definition is more specific. Pterosaurs were close relatives of dinosaurs and the first vertebrates to achieve powered flight, but they are not dinosaurs. Plesiosaurs and mosasaurs were marine reptiles from different groups, not dinosaurs either. Dimetrodon, often sold as a dinosaur toy, is actually more closely related to mammals. Understanding these differences helps you recognize how scientists group organisms based on relationships, not just appearance.

Dino Quiz & Games

Click or tap each question to reveal the answer block.

Which group includes living birds?

Answer: Theropoda within Saurischia. Birds are crown-group dinosaurs by definition.

True or false: Pterosaurs are dinosaurs.

Answer: False. Pterosaurs are pterosauromorph archosaurs, closely related but separate from Dinosauria.

What does “ornithischian” hip shape resemble?

Answer: A backward-pointing pubis is seen in many groups. Despite the name, birds are actually part of the saurischian lineage.

Which period is youngest from today: Triassic, Jurassic, or Cretaceous?

Answer: Cretaceous. (Triassic → Jurassic → Cretaceous).

How Did The Dinosaurs Go Extinct?

At the end of the Cretaceous period, dinosaurs became extinct some 66 million years ago. The most known way is that a huge asteroid hit the earth in the area that is now the Yucatán Peninsula and caused significant changes in the environment. The force must have caused dust and debris to be released to the atmosphere that caused a blockage of sunlight and a disturbance of photosynthesis, which impacted not only plants but also the animals that relied on them. Meanwhile, the volcanoes that occurred in the present-day India were highly active and could have emitted gases that caused a further alteration of the climate. All these effects resulted in a rapid ecosystem collapse. Majority of dinosaurs became extinct, yet the birds remained, and they live today as their descendants.

Glossary

Herbivore
An animal that eats plants; many ornithischians and sauropods fit here.
Carnivore
A meat-eater; classic examples include large theropods.
Omnivore
An animal that eats both plants and animals.
Paleontologist
Scientist who studies ancient life through fossils and context.
Ornithischia
Major dinosaur branch with distinctive hip structure among many members.
Saurischia
Dinosaur branch that includes sauropodomorphs and theropods (with birds).
