How Scientists Group Dinosaurs to Tell Their Story

Dinosaurs are grouped by their family, not just how they look. Here you will learn about the main types of dinosaurs, how fossils are made, and why we need to take care when finding them.

Dinosaur Types & Classification

Dinosauria nests within Archosauria. Two great dinosaur subdivisions are Saurischia (“lizard-hipped,” a historical misnomer for hip shape) and Ornithischia (“bird-hipped,” ironically not the lineage that includes living birds). Birds evolved from theropod saurischians; ornithischians include stegosaurs, ankylosaurs, ornithopods, and ceratopsians, among others.

Major groups students should recognize:

  • Theropods: mostly bipedal, includes Tyrannosaurus, dromaeosaurids, ornithomimosaurs, and birds. Teeth range from steak-knife serrations to reduced beaks.
  • Sauropodomorphs: long necks and column-like limbs in the giant forms; basal members were smaller and sometimes omnivorous. Think Diplodocus and Brachiosaurus.
  • Marginocephalia: thickened skull margins with frills (ceratopsians) or domes (pachycephalosaurs).
  • Thyreophora: armored dinosaurs such as stegosaurs with vertical plates and spikes or ankylosaurs with osteoderms and tail clubs in many species.
  • Ornithopoda: progressively sophisticated chewing, including the duck-billed hadrosaurs and the famous Iguanodon.

Fossil & Excavation Guide

How do fossils form?

Fossils are rare. Most plants and dinosaurs decay, but sometimes they get buried quickly by mud or sand. Over time, minerals can fill in the bones or replace them. Some fossils are just imprints, like footprints or leaf shapes. These can show how dinosaurs lived, even if no bones are found.

How do paleontologists work in the field?

Scientists look for fossils using maps and clues from rocks. When they find bones, they carefully cover them in plaster to protect them. They record the location, take photos, and study the layers of rock. In labs, they clean fossils and use tools and scans to study them safely.

Beginner’s guide to fossil collecting

Always follow the rules. Do not collect fossils on private land without permission. Many places do not allow collecting at all. If you are allowed to collect small fossils, write down where and when you found them. If you are unsure, take photos and contact a museum or university. Important fossils should be studied and protected, not sold.
