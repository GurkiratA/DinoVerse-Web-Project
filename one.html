Mesozoic Timeline: Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous

Dinosaurs first appeared in the Triassic, spread and evolved during the Jurassic, and became highly diverse in the Cretaceous. A mass extinction at the end of the Cretaceous wiped out all non-avian dinosaurs, likely linked to the Chicxulub impact and large-scale volcanism. Birds are modern-day dinosaurs.

Triassic Period (~252–201 million years ago)

Early dinosaurs were often small and bipedal, sharing landscapes with croc line archosaurs and other reptiles. A well-known Late Triassic example is the slender predator Coelophysis. This period set the foundation for later success. Features like upright posture, an open hip socket, and a mesotarsal ankle improved movement and allowed dinosaurs to grow larger over time.

Jurassic Period (~201–145 million years ago)

Sauropods reached enormous sizes, while stegosaurs displayed plates and spikes. Allosaurs were top predators in many northern ecosystems. The crested Dilophosaurus is known from Early Jurassic fossils, but its frill and venom are fictional. Well known examples include Brontosaurus, Diplodocus, and Allosaurus. Marine environments were home to ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs, which are not dinosaurs but closely related reptiles.

Cretaceous Period (~145–66 million years ago)

Flowering plants spread widely, and hadrosaurs and ceratopsians became highly diverse. Tyrannosaurids dominated food webs in western North America, while abelisaurids filled similar roles in Gondwana. Iconic dinosaurs like Spinosaurus, Triceratops, Ankylosaurus, and Velociraptor continue to shape public imagination.
