Dino of the Day: Tyrannosaurus rex

King of the Blockbuster

No dinosaur owns the spotlight like Tyrannosaurus rex. Franchises such as Jurassic Park and Jurassic World turned it into a global icon, but the animal behind the poster is a real Late Cretaceous predator from western North America, known from superb fossils that fuel endless research on bite force, growth, senses, and whether juveniles were fleet-footed compared to multi-ton adults.

Quick Fact File

Meaning of name: “tyrant lizard king.”
Diet: large vertebrate prey; strong evidence for bone-crushing bites and opportunistic feeding.
Period: Late Cretaceous (end of the age of non-avian dinosaurs).
Location: Hell Creek–style rocks of the United States and Canada; related tyrannosaurids across northern Laurasia.

Latest Paleontology News!

  • Feathers Keep Rewriting Theropod Looks

    New fossil evidence from small theropods is changing how scientists and artists think these dinosaurs looked. It helps explain how young tyrannosaurs and dromaeosaurs may have appeared. Feathers were likely common in many species, even if adult Tyrannosaurus had a simpler covering.

  • CT Scans Unlock Inner Ears

    Scientists use CT scans to study the inner ear structures of dinosaur skulls. This helps them understand movement, balance, and head position. These findings show that “bird-like” traits are part of a real evolutionary link.

  • Isotope Chemistry and Migration

    Chemical traces in dinosaur teeth reveal information about diet and water sources. This helps scientists track changes over time. Some large dinosaurs may have migrated seasonally, similar to modern animals searching for food.
